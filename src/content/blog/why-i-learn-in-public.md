I learn best by taking something hard apart and rebuilding it in a form I could hand to someone else.
This site collects everything that comes out of that process.

## Two ways I do it

**By hand, for model architectures.** I was learning about attention, and *Attention Is All You Need* is
the foundational paper of that whole ecosystem. Reading it, I wanted more than the equations. I wanted to
see every matrix, know its shape at every step, and do the multiplications myself until the forward pass
clicked. That became [Attention, by hand](https://guptaaditya95.github.io/llmbreakdown/attention/), and then
[BERT, by hand](https://guptaaditya95.github.io/llmbreakdown/bert/).

The part that fascinated me most was positional encoding: sine and cosine give every position its own
signature, without relying only on learned vectors. That one idea is what pushed me to build the first
breakdown.

**One framework per lesson, for leading AI.** I'm working through a course on leading AI transformation.
Rather than let the notes sit in a notebook, I turn each lesson into a framework, apply it to a case study,
and publish it in the [AI Transformation Playbook](https://guptaaditya95.github.io/ai-transformation-playbook/).

## Why publish

- **It keeps me honest.** If I can't explain it simply, I haven't understood it yet.
- **Frameworks travel better than notes.** Something you can take into a meeting tomorrow beats a page of bullet points.
- **Feedback makes the next one better.** The review of the first breakdown became the playbook for the second.
